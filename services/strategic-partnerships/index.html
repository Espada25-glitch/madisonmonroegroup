Strategic Partnership & Business Development Advisory

Make relationships part of your growth strategy.

Strategic partnership advisory helps turn a broad network or promising relationship into a defined commercial opportunity. Madison & Monroe works with owners and executives to identify fit, articulate mutual value, and plan the next step.

The decision ahead

Which relationships could create value for both sides?

An introduction is only one part of business development. Effective partnership planning considers the shared objective, each party’s contribution, and how a relationship could work in practice. The work begins with the business outcome, then evaluates the relationships that could support it.

When this work is useful

  • You see potential in complementary businesses but need a focused approach.
  • You want a more deliberate referral or collaboration strategy.
  • You need to assess a prospective partner before investing time and resources.

The work

From question to clear priorities.

01

Define the objective

Clarify the opportunity, target customers, and what a partnership would need to achieve. Establish criteria for a useful match.

02

Map and qualify partners

Research potential partners, evaluate alignment, and prioritize the most relevant relationships. Examine both the opportunity and the practical constraints.

03

Prepare the engagement

Develop a mutual-value proposition, outreach priorities, and a plan for conversations. Identify decision points and ways to assess progress.

A practical outcome

What you can take forward.

Deliverables are tailored to the agreed scope and may include:

  • A partner landscape and qualification criteria.
  • A prioritized set of opportunities with supporting rationale.
  • A partnership proposition and engagement plan.

Scope, deliverables, timing, and fees are agreed before work begins. A focused assessment can stand alone or inform a longer strategy or advisory engagement.

An illustrative business question

For a specialist service business, the work might explore complementary providers that serve a related customer need. Before outreach, the analysis would consider audience overlap, service fit, capacity, and what each business could contribute. This is an illustrative question, not a claim of an existing client engagement.

Before you begin.

Am I paying for introductions?

The engagement pays for defined advisory work: research, qualification, strategy, and engagement planning. Introductions may support that work where appropriate and mutually agreed, but are not guaranteed.

Can you work alongside existing advisors?

Yes. A scope can be designed to complement the business’s existing financial, operational, or specialist advisors. Responsibilities and the commercial objective should be clear at the outset.
