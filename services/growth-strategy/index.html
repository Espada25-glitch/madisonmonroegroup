Business Growth Strategy Consulting

A clear plan for your next stage of growth.

Business growth strategy starts with choosing which opportunities deserve your time, resources, and attention. Madison & Monroe helps business owners and executives make those choices with evidence and a practical plan.

The decision ahead

Where should the business grow next?

A new service, a new market, or a larger customer base can each create opportunity. They can also place different demands on the business. An engagement examines the commercial case alongside the resources and capabilities needed to move forward.

When this work is useful

  • You have several expansion ideas and need a way to prioritize them.
  • Demand is changing, and you want to test where your offer fits.
  • You want growth that accounts for delivery capacity and existing customers.

The work

From question to clear priorities.

01

Understand the starting point

Review the offer, customer base, commercial performance, and constraints. Agree the decision the work needs to support.

02

Evaluate the options

Compare opportunities using relevant market research, customer evidence, and the business’s ability to deliver. Identify assumptions that need testing.

03

Set a direction

Develop recommendations, sequencing, and measures of progress. Give leadership a clear basis for deciding what to do next.

A practical outcome

What you can take forward.

Deliverables are tailored to the agreed scope and may include:

  • A prioritized view of growth opportunities and their trade-offs.
  • A roadmap connecting recommended actions to the decision ahead.
  • A record of assumptions, open questions, and measures to review.

Scope, deliverables, timing, and fees are agreed before work begins. A focused assessment can stand alone or inform a longer strategy or advisory engagement.

An illustrative business question

For a professional services firm considering a new offering, the work might examine which clients would value it, how it differs from alternatives, and whether the firm has the capacity to deliver. That creates a more useful decision than pursuing expansion on demand estimates alone.

Before you begin.

Is this suitable for an owner-led business?

Yes. The scope can focus on one consequential question rather than a broad organizational review. The first conversation establishes whether the practice is a fit.

What does the engagement cost?

The fee, deliverables, and timing are agreed after the question and scope are defined, before work begins. No standard price is assumed for every business.
