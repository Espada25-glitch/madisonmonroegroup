The decision ahead

Where should your business grow next?

For owners and executive teams weighing a new market, new service, or a broader portfolio of opportunities.

Madison & Monroe provides founder-led growth strategy consulting to help compare expansion options against customer demand, economics, capabilities, and risk. The aim is a defensible decision and a practical roadmap.

01

Assess a new market

Examine target customers, alternatives, routes to market, and the capabilities required to compete. Separate evidence of demand from assumptions.

02

Evaluate a new service

Test customer need, differentiation, pricing assumptions, and delivery capacity before committing substantial resources.

03

Consider diversification

Compare adjacent opportunities with the core business. Make the trade-offs in management attention, investment, and operational complexity explicit.

The work

A disciplined path from options to decisions.

01

Establish the starting point

Review commercial performance, customer concentration, resources, and strategic objectives. Define what successful growth would mean for the business.

02

Compare the opportunities

Evaluate demand, competitive alternatives, potential economics, capability gaps, and risk. Identify the evidence needed to support or reject each opportunity.

03

Sequence the next moves

Recommend priorities, validation steps, decision points, and ownership. Build a roadmap that reflects capacity and makes uncertainty visible.

Practical outputs

A roadmap your leadership team can use.

  • An opportunity assessment comparing markets, services, or diversification options.
  • A decision framework with assumptions, evidence gaps, and trade-offs.
  • Recommendations on which opportunities to pursue, test, defer, or stop.
  • A sequenced expansion roadmap with owners, milestones, and resource requirements.
  • Measures and decision points for reviewing progress before further investment.

Deliverables, timing, fees, and responsibilities are agreed before work begins. A focused assessment may stand alone or lead into ongoing advisory.

Illustrative decision · not a client case

Should a services firm launch a new offering?

The work could examine which existing clients value the offer, how it differs from alternatives, what delivery requires, and what evidence would justify a pilot. The resulting recommendation may be to test, narrow, defer, or proceed.

Questions before you begin.

Is this suitable for a mid-sized company?

Yes, where an owner or executive team needs to evaluate expansion priorities and translate them into decisions. Scope is determined by the business question, organizational complexity, and evidence required.

Can you assess a new market or service before we invest?

Yes. The work can focus on customer demand, competing alternatives, economics, delivery requirements, and the assumptions that need testing. Findings inform an investment decision; they do not guarantee commercial success.

How do you approach diversification?

Compare each opportunity’s customer demand, connection to existing capabilities, economics, and management requirements. Identify what must be true for the opportunity to work and how to test those assumptions before scaling.

What makes an expansion roadmap practical?

It connects recommended actions to owners, milestones, resources, and decision points. It also distinguishes what can proceed now from what depends on further evidence.

How does this connect to marketing strategy?

Once the growth direction is clear, marketing strategy can define priority customers, positioning, go-to-market choices, and marketing investment.
