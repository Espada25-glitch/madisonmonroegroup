How to Get AI to Surface Your Brand

Harvard Business Review · John Gale, Luca Cian, and Luc Wathieu

As consumers ask AI systems for recommendations, brands face a new discovery challenge: being surfaced for the situations in which they are relevant. This article examines how models represent brands and how the framing of a query changes the brands that appear.

The published research examined 15 retail categories and more than 1,000 brand mentions, representing 716 unique brands. Its implications concern brand discovery; it does not establish a guaranteed formula for visibility.

Read the article at Harvard Business Review ↗

Original summary by Madison & Monroe. The complete article is available from the publisher and may require a subscription.

Implications for marketing strategy

Three questions to bring to your own business.

01

Is your identity clear?

Make the organization, expertise, and offer consistent and understandable across the website and credible external profiles.

02

Are your attributes useful?

Describe the situations you help with, whom you serve, and what the work produces. Connect customer needs to concrete service capabilities.

03

Can your claims be verified?

Support expertise with attributable research, relevant experience, and independent evidence. Distinguish published findings from business applications and hypotheses.

These are practical applications to a consulting business. The research studied retail brands; applying it to professional services should be treated as a testable strategy rather than a proven causal effect.

Research approach

Study the questions, not just the brand name.

The underlying LLMO research program examines how brand visibility changes across AI platforms and query frames, including exploratory questions, customer personas, and goal-oriented requests. Useful measures include whether a brand appears, how it is positioned, and which sources an answer cites.

For marketing and growth strategy, that suggests tracking realistic buyer questions over time, comparing the cited evidence, and improving the pages that answer those questions. A mention in one answer is not evidence of consistent visibility.
